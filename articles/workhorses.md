Some fonts want to be noticed. These would rather you noticed the words. They're dependable families for
reports, spreadsheets, slides, and long reads. They have the weights and italics a real document needs, and
most come with a sans, serif, or mono sibling, so a whole document can stay in one family.

Your iPhone or iPad's own San Francisco and New York are good workhorses already. Reach for these when you
want a document that doesn't look like a settings screen.

## Sans serifs

@font-inter: Q3 revenue up 14%. Meetings down 0%.

**Inter**, by [Rasmus Andersson](https://rsms.me/), started out in 2017 as a font for user interfaces (its
first name was Interface). It has a tall [x-height](term:x-height) for small text on screens, plus extras
that matter in a spreadsheet: tabular numbers, a slashed zero, and punctuation that adjusts to the letters
around it. A lot of the apps and websites you use every day are set in it.
[Inter Tight](font:font-inter-tight), spaced closer for headlines, is in the catalog too.

@font-roboto: Your package is out for delivery

**Roboto**, drawn by Christian Robertson at Google, has been
[Android's system font since 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roboto), so there's a decent chance you've
read more words in it than in any other font. Google describes it as having a dual nature: a mechanical
skeleton with friendly, open curves. The catalog has the whole extended family, including
[Condensed](font:font-roboto-condensed), [Flex](font:font-roboto-flex), [Slab](font:font-roboto-slab),
[Serif](font:font-roboto-serif), and [Mono](font:font-roboto-mono).

@font-open-sans: Open daily 9 – 5, closed on holidays

**Open Sans** is by Steve Matteson, who also drew Droid Sans, the font Roboto replaced on Android. It's a
[humanist](term:humanist) sans serif with open shapes and an upright, neutral-but-friendly feel, and for
years it was one of the most-used fonts on the web.

@font-source-sans-3: Agenda: 1. Coffee 2. Decisions 3. More coffee

**Source Sans 3**, by Paul D. Hunt, was Adobe's first open-source typeface, released in 2012 as Source Sans
Pro. It takes its cue from the American gothics of the early 1900s, like Franklin Gothic (see
[Inspired by the classics](article:inspired-by-the-classics)), tuned for user interfaces. The 3 arrived with
version 3, when Adobe rebuilt the family and renamed it so it wouldn't trip over documents set in the old
one.

@font-ibm-plex-sans: Think. Then write it down.

**IBM Plex Sans** replaced Helvetica Neue as [IBM's corporate typeface](https://www.ibm.com/plex/) in 2017.
Mike Abbink designed it at IBM with [Bold Monday](https://www.boldmonday.com/), a Dutch foundry, to capture
"the unique relationship between mankind and machine." It's a friendly [grotesque](term:grotesque) with
unusually expressive italics, and the family adds a [Serif](font:font-ibm-plex-serif),
a [Mono](font:font-ibm-plex-mono), a [Condensed](font:font-ibm-plex-sans-condensed), and versions for Arabic,
Hebrew, Devanagari, Thai, Japanese, Korean, and Chinese.

@font-fira-sans: Outlived the phone it was made for

**Fira Sans** was made for [Firefox OS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firefox_OS), Mozilla's phone operating
system, by Erik Spiekermann and the Berlin foundry [Carrois](https://carrois.com/). Firefox OS is long gone,
but the font stuck around. It was built to stay legible on cheap phone screens, and it comes in three
widths, plus [Fira Mono](font:font-fira-mono) and [Fira Code](font:font-fira-code), a favorite coding font.

@font-work-sans: Work hard, kern harder

**Work Sans**, by Wei Huang (who also made Fragment Mono, from the Helvetica section of
[Inspired by the classics](article:inspired-by-the-classics)), is loosely based on the grotesques of
19th-century British and German foundries. The middle weights are tuned for text on screens, and the
extremes, Hairline and Black, for headlines.

@font-geist: Deploy to production on a Friday? Bold.

**[Geist](font:font-geist)** comes from [Vercel](https://vercel.com/font), the company behind Next.js, made
with Basement Studio and inspired by Swiss design. Geist is German for "spirit" (or "ghost"). Its siblings
are [Geist Mono](font:font-geist-mono), for code, and [Geist Serif](font:font-geist-serif).

@font-figtree: SIGN UP · LOG IN · MAYBE LATER

**Figtree**, by [Erik Kennedy](https://www.erikdkennedy.com/projects/figtree.html), is a clean,
[geometric](term:geometric) sans serif made for websites and apps. It's friendly in text and still has
punch in all caps, which makes it good for buttons and short labels.

## Serifs

@font-source-serif-4: Chapter One, in which the butler is blameless

**Source Serif 4** is the serif partner to Source Sans, designed by Frank Grießhammer at Adobe. It's loosely
based on the work of [Pierre-Simon Fournier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Simon_Fournier), an
18th-century French typefounder, down to quirks like the serif at the bottom of the b.
[Optical sizes](term:optical-size) keep it sturdy in small text and refined in headings.

@font-merriweather: Minutes of the March board meeting

**Merriweather**, by Eben Sorkin of [Sorkin Type](https://sorkintype.com/), was designed to be pleasant to
read on screens: a very large x-height, slightly condensed letters, and sturdy serifs.
[Merriweather Sans](font:font-merriweather-sans) is its matching sans serif.

@font-lora: Dear Margaret, the garden is finally in bloom

**Lora**, from [Cyreal](https://cyreal.org/), is a text serif with roots in calligraphy: brushed curves
against sharp, driving serifs. It's a good pick for stories and essays that want a little warmth.

@font-literata: It was a dark and stormy night. Battery: 4%.

**Literata** started as the house font of Google Play Books, made by
[TypeTogether](https://www.type-together.com/) to read comfortably on any device. Its third version is
variable, with optical sizes that run from captions to headlines. Try it in any reading app that lets you
pick a font.

@font-libertinus-serif: Everything you read here is true. Citation needed.

**Libertinus Serif** is a fork of [Linux Libertine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_Libertine), the
typeface in Wikipedia's wordmark. It keeps Libertine's bookish look and adds wide coverage of Greek,
Cyrillic, and math symbols, which makes it a favorite for academic papers. [Libertinus Sans](font:font-libertinus) is in the catalog
too.
