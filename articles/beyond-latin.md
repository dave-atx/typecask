Your iPhone or iPad can already show nearly every writing system in the world, using Apple's own fonts. The
fonts here give you more choice of style in languages beyond English, from Chinese poetry to Persian web
pages. Most of them include Latin letters too, so a page that mixes languages still looks like one design.

## Noto

@font-noto-sans: Hello · Γειά σου · Привет · Xin chào

**[Noto](font:font-noto-sans)** is [Google's project](https://fonts.google.com/noto) to cover every script
in Unicode. The name comes from "no tofu": tofu is what type designers call the blank boxes you see when a
font is missing a character. Noto Sans covers Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic, and the catalog has over 200 other
Noto casks, most of them for a single script.

## Chinese, Japanese, and Korean

@font-noto-sans-cjk-sc: 你好，世界
= Hello, world

@font-noto-serif-cjk-jp: 吾輩は猫である。名前はまだ無い。
= I am a cat. As yet I have no name.

**[Noto Sans CJK](font:font-noto-sans-cjk-sc)** and **[Source Han Sans](font:font-source-han-sans-vf)** are
the same typeface under two names. Adobe and Google made it together and released it in 2014, and each
name's version carries about 65,000 glyphs, the most a single font file can hold. A serif version followed
in 2017, [Noto Serif CJK](font:font-noto-serif-cjk-jp) and [Source Han Serif](font:font-source-han-serif-vf).
The catalog's [JP](font:font-noto-sans-cjk-jp), [KR](font:font-noto-sans-cjk-kr),
[SC](font:font-noto-sans-cjk-sc), and [TC](font:font-noto-sans-cjk-tc) versions differ in which regional
forms of shared characters they use by default.

@font-lxgw-wenkai: 床前明月光，疑是地上霜。
= Before my bed, the bright moonlight; I took it for frost on the ground.

**LXGW WenKai** is a Chinese font in kaishu, or [regular script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_script),
the style of careful brush writing taught in school. It grew out of [Klee One](font:font-klee-one), a
Japanese font from Fontworks, which its designer extended with Simplified and Traditional Chinese
characters. He thinks it suits poetry better than long passages, so the specimen is the opening of Li Bai's
"[Quiet Night Thoughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiet_Night_Thought)."

@font-pretendard: 다람쥐 헌 쳇바퀴에 타고파
= A squirrel wants to ride an old wheel: a Korean pangram.

**Pretendard** is a Korean font built from three open-source fonts: [Inter](font:font-inter) (from
[Workhorses](article:workhorses)), Source Han Sans, and M PLUS 1p, refined for reading on screens. It comes in
nine weights and keeps Inter's Latin letters.

@font-m-plus-1: いろはにほへと ちりぬるを
= Though the colors are fragrant, they fall away.

**[M PLUS](font:font-m-plus-1)** comes from the [M+ FONTS](https://mplusfonts.github.io/) project, a family
of Japanese fonts with more than 5,700 kanji. The catalog has several versions, including
[M PLUS Rounded 1c](font:font-m-plus-rounded-1c) and coding fonts. The specimen is the start of the
[iroha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iroha), a Japanese poem that uses every kana exactly once.

## Arabic and Persian

@font-amiri: مرحبا بالعالم
= Hello, world

**Amiri** is a classical Arabic typeface in the [Naskh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naskh_%28script%29) style,
for books and other long text. It revives the type of the Bulaq Press in Cairo, known as the Amiria Press,
from the early 20th century, and is named after it.

@font-vazirmatn: سلام دنیا
= Hello, world

**Vazirmatn** began in 2015 as Vazir, a simple and legible Persian and Arabic font for websites and apps. Its
Latin letters come from [Roboto](font:font-roboto) (see [Workhorses](article:workhorses)).

## Hebrew, Devanagari, and more

@font-ibm-plex-sans-hebrew: שלום עולם
= Hello, world

**[IBM Plex Sans](font:font-ibm-plex-sans)** (see [Workhorses](article:workhorses)) has siblings for Arabic,
[Hebrew](font:font-ibm-plex-sans-hebrew), Devanagari, Thai, Japanese, Korean, and Chinese, so one design can
carry a whole multilingual project.

@font-poppins: नमस्ते दुनिया · Hello, world

**Poppins**, from the [Indian Type Foundry](https://www.indiantypefoundry.com/), was the first
[geometric](term:geometric) Devanagari typeface with a full range of weights when it came out in 2015. Ninad
Kale drew the Devanagari and Jonny Pinhorn the Latin, and the Latin has become a favorite for headlines too.

@font-gentium-plus: Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος · [en arkʰɛ̂ː ɛ̂ːn ho lóɡos]
= In the beginning was the Word

**Gentium Plus**, by Victor Gaultney at [SIL International](https://software.sil.org/gentium/), is made for
linguists and scholars: it covers Latin, Cyrillic, modern and ancient Greek, and the
[International Phonetic Alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet). Gentium is
Latin for "of the nations."
